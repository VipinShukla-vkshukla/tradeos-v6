# 2. Decision Audit

**✅ Correct Acceptance — GABRIEL (swing).** Declined 5–6 times between
10:23–10:30 IST with the identical reason "slots were already spent on
better." Two held positions (KIMS, TRAVELFOOD) closed via the give-back
guard between 11:14–12:15 IST, freeing capacity. GABRIEL entered at 12:41
IST, ~2 hours after its first decline. This is the allocator working
exactly as designed: refuse a qualifying edge only while better capital is
committed, admit it the moment a slot is real. Not a coincidence — a
mechanism.

**✅ Correct Acceptance — KIMS & TRAVELFOOD give-back exits.** Both peaked
well above where they closed (KIMS +2.15%→+1.06%, TRAVELFOOD
+2.92%→+1.31%) before the give-back guard fired. Without it, per the
codebase's own measured history (24 of 28 past losing swing trades had
been >0.5% in profit first), these were live candidates to round-trip into
losses. The guard did its job on its first real day of being exercised.

**❌ False Acceptance (execution, not decision) — the give-back sells were
blocked at the broker.** Both automated SELL orders were rejected: `order
PERMANENTLY BLOCKED for KIMS SELL — IP (103.197.74.141) is not allowed to
place orders for this app`, same for TRAVELFOOD. The *decision* was
correct; the *execution* failed. Both positions still closed profitably
(`exit_reason: BROKER_EXIT`) at prices well above the resting GTT stop
levels — meaning a human, almost certainly reading the Telegram give-back
alert, sold manually rather than the system doing it. Had nobody been
watching, TRAVELFOOD's guard re-fired **four times** over 20 minutes
without the sell landing, which is exactly the shape of "the automation
cannot be trusted unattended right now."

**✅ Correct Rejection (tentative) — every SDN short today (25 distinct
setups, 0 taken until late).** `intraday/shortability.py` and the SDN
short family were built and shipped *last night* — today was their first
live exposure. 19 were refused by the shortability gate (liquidity/
circuit-proxy/runway), 6 by the regime gate (index not confirming weakness
under a NEUTRAL, low-VIX day). Two eventually cleared everything on
SAPPHIRE and were taken — both lost (aggregate −₹51.90, −0.63R). One day,
two trades: not evidence the gate is right *or* wrong, just its first data
point.

**❌ False Rejection — none identified with confidence.** No blocked
setup's `outcome` column showed a clear TARGET that the system missed by
refusing it. The closest candidates (BLOCKED_STRUCTURE ×2, BLOCKED_REENTRY
×1) don't have enough resolved-outcome detail in one day to call either
way.

# 3. Pattern Discovery

**Market Regime.** NEUTRAL, regime score 56/100, India VIX 11.87 (calm),
breadth constructive (59% of names above 200-DMA), momentum sub-score weak
(6/20 — Nifty +0.9% over 20 days). DII strongly net-buying (+₹35,774cr
over 20 days) against FII net-selling (−₹7,817cr over 20 days) but FII
net-*buying* the last 5 days (+₹2,980cr) — a market being quietly
absorbed, not trending. This is the backdrop against which shorts got
refused: a calm, domestically-supported tape gives the short gates little
"confirmed weakness" to work with.

**Allocator behaviour.** Swing's live allocator (`alloc_live_swing=true`)
evaluated 17 distinct candidates today: 6 TAKE, 11 DECLINE — and of the 11
declines, most (CAPLIPOINT 0.040 vs 0.044, TVSMOTOR 0.040 vs 0.044,
JSWSTEEL 0.040 vs 0.041, TBOTEK 0.039 vs 0.043) missed by a narrow margin,
not a wide one. The binding constraint today was **slot count**
(`swing_max_new_per_day`), not signal scarcity — there was more qualifying
edge than capacity. Intraday's allocator remains shadow-only
(`alloc_live_intraday=false`); its hurdle values run deeply negative (−1.3
to −1.6) against swing's positive small-decimal hurdle, a different scale
entirely — expected, since it's never been calibrated on live enforcement.

**Timing.** 63 of 78 intraday setups fired in PRIME phase, 10 in DRIFT, 5
in AFTERNOON — activity concentrated early, as expected for opening-range
and gap-based engines (ORB, GAP made up the bulk of taken trades).

**Risk/Reward on what was actually taken (intraday).** All 5 non-SDN taken
trades were LONG, R:R 2.0–2.8 planned. Outcomes: 2 STOP (both ~−1.05R,
close to plan), 2 TIMEOUT (both barely positive, +0.01R to +0.71R — time
stop capturing a stall, not a loss), 1 TARGET (GVT&D, +1.46%). Nothing
here broke its own risk plan; losses were plan-sized, not surprises.

**Institutional/execution risk.** The IP-allowlist rejection is a repeat
of a *known category* (Kite's allowlist), but not the same specific bug as
before (`_force_ipv4()` guards against IPv6-first resolution; today's
block was a genuine IPv4 address, `103.197.74.141`, simply not on the list
— likely a changed network/ISP lease, or — per `deploy/validate_server.py`
— an Oracle Cloud ephemeral public IP that changed on an instance
stop/start). Different root cause, same symptom: automated exits silently
unable to execute while every other readiness signal looks fine.

**Cost friction on small CNC clips.** KIMS: +₹8.65 gross became **−₹7.23
after charges** (₹15.88 CNC round-trip cost on an ₹803 position).
TRAVELFOOD survived charges (+₹34.80 → +₹16.91) only because the qty was
2, not 1. This is the same friction problem the codebase's own comments
already name (`sizing_max_cost_r` currently disabled because "the clip
size is the problem, not the gate") — today is a fresh, live confirmation
of it, not a new discovery.

# 5. Initial TradeOS Knowledge Base

*(First session — full categories below. From tomorrow, this section is a
diff against `knowledge_base/KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`.)*

### Validated Rules
- **The give-back guard protects real profit.** Two live swing positions
  that peaked +2.15% and +2.92% were sold at +1.06% and +1.31% instead of
  round-tripping further. *Evidence:* KIMS, TRAVELFOOD, 6 Aug.
  *Confidence:* Medium-high — this is the mechanism's designed behaviour
  confirmed once live; the codebase's own 70-trade historical study is the
  deeper evidence base. *More evidence needed:* several more live
  give-back triggers to confirm the exit price consistently beats letting
  it ride to the stop.
- **The swing allocator enforces slot scarcity correctly, not
  arbitrarily.** GABRIEL's repeated decline-then-accept cycle traces
  cleanly to slot availability, not a flaky bar. *Evidence:* full decision
  log, 6 Aug. *Confidence:* Medium. *More evidence needed:* a session
  where slots are NOT the binding constraint, to see the edge/hurdle
  comparison do the work alone.

### Promising Hypotheses
- **Automated live-order execution cannot yet be trusted unattended.** Two
  real orders were blocked by a broker-side config issue that every
  readiness check missed until the order itself failed. *Evidence:*
  IP-allowlist rejection, 6 Aug, one session. *Confidence:* Low-medium
  (n=1 session, but the failure mode is exactly the documented
  IP-allowlist category). *More evidence needed:* whether this recurs on
  sessions with a stable IP, to separate "occasional network hiccup" from
  "allowlist genuinely stale."

### Ideas Requiring More Evidence
- **SDN short engine viability.** 2 trades, both losses, first day ever
  live. *Confidence:* None yet. *Evidence needed:* ~20 resolved outcomes
  spanning ≥10 sessions (per `MIN_SAMPLE`/`MIN_SESSIONS`) before any
  verdict.
- **Whether `swing_max_new_per_day` (currently limiting entries, not
  edge, today) should change.** *Confidence:* None yet. *Evidence
  needed:* several more sessions in similar regime to see if
  slot-exhaustion is typical or today was unusual.

### Rules That Did Not Work Today
- None identified as *rules* failing — today's shortfalls (blocked
  automated sells, SDN's 0-for-2) are either execution/infrastructure
  issues or too small a sample to call a rule failure. Flagging this
  explicitly rather than forcing an item into this bucket to fill it.

# 6. Final Verdict

**Did TradeOS make good trading decisions today?** Yes, on the decisions
it actually controlled. The give-back guard, the allocator's slot
discipline, and the shortability gate all behaved as designed on their
first real day of meaningful activity. The one clear failure was not a
trading decision — it was infrastructure (a broker order got blocked)
sitting directly downstream of two correct decisions.

**Biggest mistake:** Not a trading call — the IP allowlist blocking both
live give-back exits. The system's own risk logic did its job and would
have gone uncredited if a human hadn't been watching Telegram at the right
moment.

**Best decision:** The give-back guard on KIMS and TRAVELFOOD. Two
positions that had round-tripped over half their peak gain were closed
with profit still on the table, converting what the codebase's own history
says is usually a losing pattern into two small live wins.

**If only ONE improvement could be made before tomorrow's session:**
Confirm the current outbound IP is on Zerodha's order-placement allowlist
before market open — today's correct decisions only survived because a
human was available to execute them by hand.
